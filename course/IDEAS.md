# Idea intake

Every student idea receives a reasoned placement under the [charter](CHARTER.md). The instructor connects curiosity to learning goals and protects the essential outcomes. A topic request is not automatically a new milestone, dated assignment or compute authorization.

## Integration procedure

1. Capture the question or desired artifact, its motivation, related milestone criteria and prerequisites.
2. Define the smallest useful investigation, its focused hours and compute needs, and what evidence would answer the question. Look for an existing exercise it could replace.
3. Assign one disposition from the table below. Explain the fit, time source, displaced work and next opportunity to the learner.
4. If the placement needs more time than the learner has chosen, moves a date, adds a required milestone or incurs spending, present the concrete trade-off and let the learner decide. Otherwise fit it within the existing window and report the adjustment.
5. Keep live tasks, actual hours and results in the learner's progress tracker. At completion or the next window review, record the outcome, any earned core credit and the remaining question. Preserve earlier decisions when a placement changes.

| Disposition | Boundary and scheduling rule |
|---|---|
| Integrate now | A bounded exploration uses the agreed exploration budget and has satisfied prerequisites. An early overview may precede technical readiness if its limits are explicit. |
| Substitute an exercise | The alternative demonstrates the same required criterion, difficulty and independent understanding within the agreed time/resources. Record the replaced exercise; count evidence and hours once. |
| Defer | Name the prerequisite or revisit event and a suitable future module. Deferral has an explicit next opportunity, not an unexplained backlog entry. |
| Propose a milestone | The idea adds a distinct assessable capability not already covered. Specify prerequisites, criteria, materials, projects, hours and schedule impact; add it only after agreement. Preserve existing milestone IDs and required outcomes. |
| Route elsewhere | Explain which portion, if any, belongs here; name the more suitable course/project when known. Routing does not authorize creating or assigning work in another workspace. |

The learner decides how much time goes to exploration. Prefer one investigation at a time; a larger investigation is split, deferred or presented with a schedule trade-off. An independent exploration project needs its own bounded owner and must fit the one-active-project limit. Small explorations can live within the current execution owner.

## Intake record

For each new idea, record: stable ID; original request; related criteria; prerequisite evidence; smallest question/deliverable; estimated focused/compute hours; disposition and reason; time source or substituted exercise; approval needed and its status; linked execution owner when assigned; revisit trigger; outcome and evidence when available. Estimate and measured hours remain separate.

## Placement examples

- A different tokenizer can replace P02 if it still supports the required segmentation, round-trip and failure analysis.
- An early MoE question can receive a short architecture overview, with its technical claim investigation deferred to L10 after the relevant prerequisites.
- A chatbot idea can contribute a bounded L5 fine-tuning experiment; authentication and billing are routed outside this course.
- A request for a full new model modality starts as a placement discussion. A new required milestone needs its own assessable outcome and explicit time trade-off.

These examples illustrate the policy; they are not new student requests or tasks.
